Every week brings a new headline about AI democratizing software. "Now anyone can build an app." "The barrier to entry is gone."

But the barrier was never the code.

## Access isn't the bottleneck

The hard part of building software was never typing the syntax. It was knowing what to build, for whom, and why it matters. It was the judgment to say no to features, the taste to know a bad idea when you have it, and the persistence to ship something no one asked for and make them want it.

AI made the typing cheaper. It didn't make the judgment better.

## What actually produces builders

Desire. The kind of desire that makes someone stay up late debugging because they *need* the thing to exist. AI can't manufacture that. It can only lower the cost of acting on it.

Which is why we're seeing more tools and roughly the same number of builders. The bottleneck moved. It just didn't move to where the headlines said it would.
