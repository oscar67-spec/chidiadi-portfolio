Most people treat AI automation like a magic wand. You prompt it, it works, you move on.

I built 12 AI workflows in a single month. Only 3 are still running today. The other 9 were abandoned within two weeks.

Here is the autopsy.

## The pattern

The workflows that died shared a few traits:

1. **They solved a problem no one actually had.** I built them because I *could*, not because someone asked. A beautiful automation that nobody uses is just a complicated way to procrastinate.
2. **They required daily human babysitting.** If an agent needs you to check it every morning to make sure it didn't hallucinate a lead or send a weird message, it hasn't automated anything. It's just given you a new chore.
3. **They broke the moment an API changed.** No error handling, no retries, no fallback. One 401 and the whole chain collapses.

## What survived

The 3 that are still running all have something in common: they connect directly to revenue or time saved, and they run unattended.

- **X Lead Finder:** An automated pipeline that sources leads on X and drafts personalized outreach. It runs on a schedule, logs results to a sheet, and pings me only when a lead meets a qualification threshold.
- **WhatsApp Sales Agent:** An end-to-end sales assistant that handles conversations, qualifies prospects, and books calls. It has guardrails: when it's unsure, it hands off to a human.
- **Google Maps Business Scraper:** A high-efficiency scraping workflow for local business intelligence and lead generation, delivering clean data without manual copy-pasting.

## The lesson

AI automation isn't about building the most impressive workflow. It's about building the *boring* one, the one that quietly works in the background while you do something else.

If you're building AI agents, ask yourself before you start: will this still be running in two weeks? If the answer isn't a confident yes, don't build it yet.
