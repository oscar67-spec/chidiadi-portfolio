Before I developed a content system, I treated every post as if it required a brand new life experience.

I'd sit down to write and wait. Wait for the right mood. Wait for a flash of insight. Wait for something "worthy" to happen to me so I'd have something to say.

That's not a writing practice. That's a hobby.

## The shift

When I started ghostwriting for B2B SaaS and AI founders, I didn't have the luxury of waiting for inspiration. Founders need consistency. They need to show up in their audience's feed not when they feel like it, but when their prospects are scrolling.

So I built a system:

1. **Capture, don't create.** Every conversation with a founder, every support ticket, every sales objection becomes a raw note. I don't write posts from scratch. I write from what's already been said.
2. **One idea per post.** A post isn't an essay. It's one observation, one argument, one "what if." That makes it finishable.
3. **Batch the thinking, separate the writing.** I spend Monday collecting raw material. Tuesday I outline. Wednesday I draft. Thursday I edit. Never all in one day.

## What the system actually does

It doesn't make writing easier. It makes writing *reliable*. The founder stays focused on the product. I handle the words. Their voice stays consistent. Their audience stops scrolling.

That's the job of a ghostwriter: to turn expertise into clear, coherent messaging: on a deadline.
