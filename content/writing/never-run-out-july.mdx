The most common lie writers tell themselves: "I have nothing to write about."

What they usually mean is "I don't have a *new* thing to write about." And that's a different problem, one you don't actually have.

## One experience, many pieces

A single lived experience can become dozens of pieces if you stop trying to find new material and start extracting more from the material you already have.

The meeting that went badly. The project that failed. The thing a client said that you can't stop thinking about. Each one contains a dozen angles: the lesson, the framework, the story, the counterargument, the behind-the-scenes, the mistake.

## The shift

Stop asking "what should I write about?" Start asking "what did I already live through, and which lens haven't I used yet?"

This piece was the seed of what I later formalized as the 4 Lenses Framework. Same idea, longer version.
