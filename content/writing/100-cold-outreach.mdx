I sent out a hundred cold messages this week. Not a hundred *sent*, a hundred genuinely researched, personally written, specific messages.

Here's what I learned.

## Volume is not the lever (until it is)

Everyone says "personalize at scale," which is usually a contradiction. You can have volume or you can have specificity, until you build a system that makes specificity cheap. That's what AI is actually good for in outreach: not writing more messages, but making each message *specific* faster.

## What worked

- **Specific observations beat clever hooks.** The messages that got replies mentioned something only someone who read their work would know.
- **Short beat long.** Three sentences outperformed three paragraphs.
- **The follow-up mattered more than the first message.** Most replies came on touch two.

## What I'd never do again

Mass-send a template with a name swapped in. It felt efficient and it converted at almost zero. The lesson: the work you skip is usually the work that works.
