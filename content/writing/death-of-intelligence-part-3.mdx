This is the third and final part of *The Death of Intelligence*. Parts one and two looked at the problem: what happens to human thinking when the answer is always one prompt away.

This part is about the antidotes.

## Antidote 1: Make the thinking visible

If the answer arrives without the process, the skill never forms. In education especially, the goal isn't to ban AI, it's to require the reasoning to be shown. The prompt, the dead ends, the revision.

## Antidote 2: Deliberate friction

Not every task should be optimized. Some things should stay slow on purpose: the first draft, the hard problem set, the argument you have to build yourself. Friction is where competence is forged.

## Antidote 3: Teach judgment, not recall

AI made recall cheap. What it can't make cheap is judgment: knowing which answer to trust, which source to doubt, which trade-off is worth it. That's the skill worth teaching now.
